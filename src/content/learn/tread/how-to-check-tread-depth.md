## Two coins, one gauge, five minutes

You can check your tread with a penny from the cup holder. It just won't tell you everything.

There are three ways to check tread depth at home: the **penny test**, the **quarter test** and a **tread depth gauge**. Each one answers a different question. Here's what each shows, how to do it, and which lines matter.

## The two lines that matter: 2/32 and 4/32

In the US, tread depth is measured in **32nds of an inch**, from the top of the tread to the bottom of the main grooves.

| Line | About | Who draws it | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2/32** | 1.6 mm | NHTSA | Replace the tire. The wear bars are flush with the tread. |
| **4/32** | 3.2 mm | AAA | Replace for wet roads. Stopping distances grow sharply. |

The 2/32 line is the floor. The 4/32 line is where AAA recommends replacing, based on its 2018 wet-braking study: at 60 mph, tires worn to 4/32 took an average of **87 feet longer** to stop a passenger car than new tires did. Consumer Reports makes the same point about worn tires in the wet. In a South Florida rainy season, 4/32 is the line to plan around.

Our [tread depth chart](/learn/tread/tread-depth) covers every depth in between. This guide is about measuring.

## Method 1: the penny test (checks 2/32)

NHTSA and USTMA give the same instructions:

1. Hold a penny with **Lincoln's head upside down**, facing you.
2. Push it into a main groove until the edge touches the bottom.
3. Look at his head. If you can see **the top of Lincoln's head**, the tread is at or below 2/32, and NHTSA says to replace the tire.

If part of his head is hidden by the tread, you're above 2/32 at that spot. That's all it tells you: above the floor or not.

## Method 2: the quarter test (checks 4/32)

Same move, bigger coin:

1. Hold a quarter with **Washington's head pointing down** into the groove.
2. Push it in until it touches the bottom.
3. If you can see **all of Washington's head**, you're at about 4/32 or less. Time to start shopping.

Bridgestone's tread guide describes the quarter test this way, as a check for the 4/32 line on wet roads. If the tread covers part of his head, you have more than 4/32 at that spot.

Coins are quick, but they're pass-or-fail checks at one line. Treat them as a screen, not a measurement.

## Method 3: a tread depth gauge (gives you a number)

A tread depth gauge is a small probe that reads in 32nds of an inch, and often millimeters too. It's the only one of the three methods that gives you a number you can write down and compare next month.

How to use one:

1. **Push the probe in** so it sticks out past the base.
2. **Set the tip into a main groove**, not a thin slit in the tread block.
3. **Press the base flat** against the tread blocks on either side of the groove.
4. **Read the depth**, then pull the gauge out without touching the probe.

## The built-in check: wear bars

You don't even need a coin to spot the 2/32 line. NHTSA describes **treadwear indicators**: small raised bars molded across the bottom of the grooves. Every tire has them.

- Look across the tread for short, flat bridges running between the ribs.
- If the tread surface is **flush with a bar** anywhere, the tire is at 2/32 there and it's time to replace it.

Wear bars only show the floor. By the time they're flush, the tire is already past AAA's 4/32 line.

## How to check tread depth, step by step

1. **Park on level ground** and turn the front wheels fully to one side so you can see the whole tread.
2. **Check three grooves on every tire**: inside, center and outside.
3. **Check at two or three spots** around each tire. Roll the car a few feet if you need to.
4. **Go by the shallowest reading.** A tire is only as deep as its most worn groove.
5. **Look for the wear bars** while you're there.
6. **Write it down** by position: front left, front right, rear left, rear right.
7. **Check the spare** if it's a full-size tire that's part of your rotation.

Do it monthly, alongside the pressure check. Our guide on [how to check tire pressure](/learn/pressure/how-to-check-tire-pressure) covers the other half of the routine.

## What your readings are telling you

**Even and above 4/32.** Keep checking monthly.

**At or near 4/32.** AAA recommends replacing here. Start shopping before the rainy season, not during it.

**At 2/32, or flush with the wear bars.** NHTSA says to replace the tire.

**Uneven across one tire.** If the inside groove reads 3/32 and the outside reads 6/32, something is wearing the tire unevenly. Inflation, alignment and suspension are possible causes, and new tires will wear the same way if the cause isn't found. Have a technician inspect it.

**Different from front to rear.** Note it, and keep the tires on your rotation schedule. See the [rotation pattern for your car](/tire-rotation-pattern).

## Tread isn't the whole story

Deep tread doesn't clear a tire on its own. While you're down there, look for cuts, bulges and cracks in the tread and sidewalls; NHTSA's advice is to have any damage looked at by a tire professional. And check the date code, because a tire can have plenty of tread and still be old. Our guide on [how long tires last](/learn/tread/how-long-do-tires-last) covers miles and years together.
