## What the numbers mean

US tread depth is measured in 32nds of an inch. A typical new passenger tire
starts around 10/32 in. Per [NHTSA](https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle-safety/tires),
tires have built-in tread wear indicator bars that show when the tread is down
to 2/32 in.

| Depth (in) | Depth (mm) | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 10/32 | 7.9 | Keep checking monthly |
| 6/32 | 4.8 | Keep checking monthly |
| 4/32 | 3.2 | Consider replacing |
| 2/32 | 1.6 | Replace |

## How to check it

1. Find the wear bars in the main grooves.
2. Measure three grooves across the tire: outer, center and inner.
3. Use the shallowest reading, and have the tire inspected if the readings differ a lot.

You can also use the free [tire check tool](/tools/tire-check) or read the
guide on [installation](/install).

### A note on units

To convert 32nds of an inch to millimeters, multiply by 25.4 and divide by 32.
