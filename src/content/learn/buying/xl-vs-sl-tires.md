## Two versions of the same size

Look up a common crossover size like 235/55R19 and you'll often find it listed twice: once with a plain load index, and once with a higher load index followed by **XL**.

- **SL, standard load.** The default. Most tires don't print "SL" at all; no load marking means standard load.
- **XL, extra load.** Toyo and Continental both describe extra load, or reinforced, tires the same way: they're built to carry **more load at a higher inflation pressure** than a standard load tire of the same size. You may also see "Extra Load" or "Reinforced" on the sidewall for the same idea.

That second half of the sentence, *at a higher inflation pressure*, is the part most people miss.

## Where the extra capacity comes from

An XL tire isn't simply a tougher version of the same tire. Its construction is reinforced so that it can **run at higher pressure**, and the higher pressure is what lets it carry more weight.

Toyo's guidelines for using load and inflation tables give these reference points for P-metric passenger tires:

| | Reference pressure for maximum load |
|---|---|
| Standard load (SL) | 35 psi |
| Extra load (XL) | 41 psi |

The consequence: an XL tire's extra capacity is tied to that **higher** pressure. Toyo describes XL tires as carrying additional load at a higher inflation pressure, and Continental notes that reinforced tires are designed to run at higher pressure than standard load tires. Run an XL tire at a standard load pressure and you aren't using the capacity it's rated for.

So a car specified with XL tires at its placard pressure is relying on that construction. Swap in standard load tires of the same size and the load capacity can fall short of what the vehicle maker designed for.

### The sidewall number is a ceiling, not a target

Every tire has its maximum load molded near the bead, along with the pressure that load applies to, in a line such as "Max. load 1,201 lbs at" a stated psi. Toyo's replacement bulletin points to this marking as the way to compare tires.

That maximum pressure is **not** the pressure to run. Your target is the cold pressure on the vehicle's door placard, unless an installer has worked out a different one from the tire maker's tables because the tire type or size changed.

## Load index: the number that has to match

The load index is the number right after the size, like the **104** in 235/55R19 104V XL. Michelin's load rating guide covers how each index maps to a weight per tire.

USTMA's rule for replacements is to use a load index equal to or above what your vehicle placard requires, and Toyo's bulletin says a replacement should carry at least the same maximum load as the original. When the original was XL, that usually means the replacement has to be XL too, because the standard load version of the size has a lower load index.

## Why it matters on EVs, SUVs and crossovers

XL tires show up most on vehicles that are heavy for their tire size.

**EVs.** Battery packs add weight without making the car any bigger. Michelin's EV guidance explains that some EVs need tires rated for more load than XL can give at normal pressures, which is why the HL class below exists. Our [Tesla tire basics guide](/learn/tesla/tesla-tire-basics) covers this for Tesla models specifically.

**SUVs and crossovers.** Heavier vehicles on passenger-car tire sizes are where XL versions of a size tend to appear. Your door placard, not the vehicle's type, tells you whether yours calls for one.

### HL: the next step up

There's now a load class above XL. Michelin explains that **HL (high load)** tires are engineered to stay the same relative size as a passenger tire but carry a heavier load: on average, more than an XL tire of the same size at the same pressure. Michelin notes the HL standard was adopted in Europe in 2021 and in the U.S. in 2023.

If your placard or current tires show HL, the replacement needs to be HL. An XL tire in the same size isn't a like-for-like substitute.

## Switching between SL and XL

**From XL to SL.** If your vehicle maker specifies XL, don't. The load index drops below the original.

**From SL to XL.** Usually possible, but it isn't automatic. The XL tire's extra capacity comes with its higher pressure, so you're not gaining capacity unless the pressure changes, and a higher pressure changes how the car rides. Toyo's replacement bulletin warns about the opposite risk: fitting tires that carry less than the originals, or failing to adjust pressure so the new tires carry the original load. The answer for any change is the same: the installer looks up the right pressure in the tire maker's load and inflation table.

**Different size at the same time.** If you're also plus-sizing, run the numbers first with the [plus size calculator](/plus-size-calculator), then match the load.

## Quick reference

| You see | It means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| No load marking | Standard load (SL) | Match or exceed the placard load index |
| XL, Extra Load, Reinforced | Extra load: more capacity at higher pressure | Replace with XL if the vehicle specifies it |
| HL | High load: more than XL at the same size and pressure | Replace with HL if the vehicle specifies it |
| LT with a load range letter | Light truck tire | See our [LT vs P-metric guide](/learn/buying/lt-vs-p-metric) |
