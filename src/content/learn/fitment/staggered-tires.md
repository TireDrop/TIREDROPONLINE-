## Square vs staggered

Most cars are **square**: the same tire size on all four wheels. A **staggered** car runs **different sizes front and rear**, usually with wider tires at the back, and often wider or different-diameter wheels there too.

Michelin describes staggered fitments as common on rear-wheel-drive performance cars and some sports models, where the rear axle handles more power. Some EVs are staggered too, as our Tesla guides cover for certain Tesla models.

Here's how a staggered door placard might read:

| Axle | Size on the placard |
|---|---|
| Front | 245/40R19 |
| Rear | 275/35R19 |

That's an example of the format, not a recommendation for any car. Your door-jamb sticker lists the exact pair for yours.

## How to tell if your car is staggered

1. **Read the door-jamb placard.** A staggered car lists two sizes, one for the front axle and one for the rear.
2. **Compare the sidewalls.** Look at one front tire and one rear tire. If the sizes differ, you're staggered.
3. **Check the owner's manual.** Some cars came staggered on certain trims or wheel packages and square on others.

The [Tire Size Finder](/tire-size-finder) reads both sizes straight off the door-jamb sticker. When it sees two sizes, it keeps both, so you shop front and rear separately.

## Rotation: what you can and can't do

This is the biggest practical difference from a square car. Michelin, Tire Rack and other tire retailers' fitment guides describe the same limits.

**No axle-to-axle rotation.** The front and rear tires are different sizes, so they can't swap axles.

**Side-to-side, if non-directional.** If the tires don't have a rotation arrow, they can usually swap from left to right on the same axle.

**No rotation, if directional.** Directional tires have to keep rolling the way the arrow points. Swap them side to side and they'd roll backwards, unless they're dismounted and remounted on the wheels. Tire Rack notes that on a staggered car with directional tires, each tire's position is effectively fixed.

## What it means for wear

On a square car, rotation spreads wear across all four tires. On a staggered car, the front and rear pairs stay on their own axles for life.

Retailers' staggered fitment guides spell out the result: because staggered tires can't be rotated front to back, wear can be less even, and you may replace them sooner, depending on your drivetrain, alignment and driving. In practice that means:

- **Check tread depth per axle.** Don't assume the fronts and rears wear together. Our guide to [tread depth](/learn/tread/tread-depth) shows how to measure it.
- **Expect to replace pairs at different times.** The rears and fronts can reach the end of their tread at different mileages.
- **Keep alignment in check.** Uneven wear that rotation would normally even out stays where it starts.

### Warranty terms can differ

Treadwear warranties are written with rotation in mind. Some tire makers set different terms when sizes differ front and rear. **Pirelli's U.S. limited warranty**, for example, says that on vehicles with different replacement tire sizes front and rear, treadwear coverage for the rear tires is **50% of the stated mileage**.

Other makers write their own terms. Read the warranty booklet for the specific tire before you rely on a mileage figure.

## Buying staggered tires: 2+2

You're buying two sizes, so you're really buying two pairs.

**Match the sizes to the placard.** Front size on the front, rear size on the rear. Load index and speed rating should meet or beat what the placard lists for each axle.

**Keep the same tire model on both axles.** Michelin recommends four matching tires where possible, the same brand, model, load index and speed rating, and at minimum matching pairs on each axle. On a staggered car, that means the same model in two sizes. Mixing models front and rear gives the two axles different grip.

**Replace in pairs per axle.** Tires on the same axle should always match. If only the rears are worn, a rear pair in the same model is the usual route.

**All-wheel drive needs extra care.** Michelin notes that AWD and 4WD systems are sensitive to differences in tire circumference between positions. On a staggered AWD car, the vehicle maker sets the front and rear sizes so the rolling diameters work together. Check your owner's manual for its rules on tread depth differences and matching before replacing only one pair. Our [Tesla rotation and alignment guide](/learn/tesla/tesla-rotation-alignment-tpms) shows how one maker handles this.

### Going square instead

Some owners ask about running one size all around so they can rotate. On many staggered cars the **wheels themselves differ** front and rear, and on AWD cars the sizes are set by design. Treat it as a question for the owner's manual and a fitment check, not a shortcut.
