## No top-five list here

Search for the best tires for a Tesla and you'll find ranked lists. This isn't one. We don't rank brands or products, and a list that ignores your size, your driving and what you care about most isn't much use anyway.

What this guide does is explain the **categories** of tire that make sense on a Tesla in South Florida, name EV-specific lines as examples from the tire makers' own pages, and show you how to weigh the four things that matter most here: **wet grip, tread life, noise and range**.

One rule comes before all of it: the tire has to match your Tesla's **size, load index and speed rating**, front and rear. Our [Tesla factory tire sizes](/learn/tesla/tesla-tire-sizes) guide shows where to find them.

## What South Florida asks of a tire

Snow isn't part of the picture. What South Florida driving actually throws at a tire is:

- **Rain.** Heavy afternoon storms put standing water on I-95, the Turnpike and the Sawgrass. Wet braking and water clearance matter more here than winter grip.
- **Heat.** Hot pavement and summer sun raise tire temperature and pressure. Our guide to [Florida heat and tires](/learn/florida/florida-heat-tires) covers what NHTSA says about heat and aging.
- **Long, flat highway miles,** where noise and range are noticeable every day.

On a Tesla, add the EV demands from our [Tesla tire basics](/learn/tesla/tesla-tire-basics) guide: extra weight, instant torque and a quiet cabin.

## The tire categories that fit a Tesla here

### EV-specific touring and all-season tires

These are built around what EVs ask of a tire. Examples from the makers' own pages:

- **Hankook iON evo AS.** Hankook describes it as a grand touring all-season tire for EV drivers, designed for EV weight and lower rolling resistance.
- **Bridgestone Turanza EV.** Bridgestone describes it as a grand touring tire for premium EVs, using its ENLITEN technology to reduce rolling resistance, with a tread designed to reduce road noise.
- **Goodyear ElectricDrive 2.** Goodyear describes it as an all-season EV tire with improved rolling resistance and SoundComfort foam to reduce cabin noise, offered in sizes for Tesla models among others.
- **Pirelli Elect.** Rather than one tire, Pirelli uses an **Elect** marking on versions of its P Zero, Scorpion and Cinturato lines developed for electric vehicles, focused on rolling resistance, noise and grip.
- **Continental.** Continental marks tire models designed for EVs with an **EV-Compatible** logo on the sidewall.

These names are examples, not recommendations. Availability depends on your exact size.

### Regular all-season tires

A standard all-season tire can fit a Tesla as long as it matches the size, load index (including any XL or HL marking) and speed rating. It may not have foam or EV-specific tuning, so it can be louder or use a little more range. Check the tire maker's page for those details.

### Summer performance tires

Michelin says summer tires are designed for warm conditions, above 45°F, and deliver their best grip and handling on dry and wet roads in that range. Below 45°F, Michelin says the compound hardens and grip drops on every surface. In South Florida, most driving is well above that. If you take the car north in winter, factor it in.

The EV example here is the **Michelin Pilot Sport EV**, which Michelin describes as engineered for electric sports cars, with low rolling resistance and Michelin Acoustic foam. Some factory Tesla packages also come on summer tires.

## How to weigh wet grip, tread life, noise and range

You can't max out all four. Michelin is direct about it: carmakers often prioritize range when optimizing original-equipment EV tires, and that comes with trade-offs such as less grip or greater tire wear. So decide what matters most to you, then read each tire's specs with that in mind.

| What you care about | What to look at | Where to find it |
|---|---|---|
| **Wet grip** | UTQG traction grade (AA, A, B, C) and the tire's category | Sidewall; tire maker's page |
| **Tread life** | UTQG treadwear grade, compared within one brand; mileage warranty terms | Sidewall; tire maker's page |
| **Noise** | Acoustic foam; EV-specific tuning | Tire maker's page for your exact size |
| **Range** | Whether the maker says it's designed for low rolling resistance | Tire maker's page |
| **Fit** | Size, load index, speed rating | Door placard; owner's manual |

A few notes on reading those numbers, per NHTSA's UTQG guide:

- The **traction grade** comes from a straight-line braking test on wet pavement. It doesn't measure cornering grip or hydroplaning.
- The **treadwear grade** is assigned by the manufacturer and is most useful for comparing tires from the same brand.
- The **temperature grade** (A, B, C) reflects a tire's resistance to heat.

Our [UTQG guide](/learn/sidewall/utqg-ratings) explains all three in detail.

### Tread depth matters more than the label in the rain

Whatever you buy, wet grip fades as tread wears. Our [tread depth chart](/learn/tread/tread-depth) explains the 2/32 inch replacement line and why Consumer Reports' testing supports considering replacement at 4/32 inch for wet roads. On a Tesla, which tends to wear tires faster, measure every tire at each rotation.

## Keep pressure right in the heat

The best tire for the job still needs the right pressure. Tire pressure rises with temperature and falls when it cools, so a reading in a hot driveway at 3 p.m. isn't the same as one at 7 a.m. Set pressure cold, to the figure on your Tesla's door placard, and check it monthly. Under-inflated tires also roll with more resistance, which costs range.
