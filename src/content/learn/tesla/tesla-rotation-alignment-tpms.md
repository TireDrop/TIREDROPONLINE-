## Rotation, alignment and TPMS work together

On a Tesla, three bits of routine service decide how long a set of tires lasts and whether the dash warnings mean what they should:

- **Rotation** moves tires between positions so they wear evenly.
- **Alignment** keeps the wheels pointed the way they should so no tire scrubs along one edge.
- **TPMS**, the tire pressure monitoring system, has to know which sensor sits on which wheel after tires move.

Tesla's owner's manuals cover all three. Here's what they say, and where a tire shop takes over.

## How often Tesla says to rotate

Tesla's owner's manuals for the Model 3, Model Y and Cybertruck give the same rule, and it's the same figure our [Model Y tire buying guide](/blog/tesla-model-y-tires-guide) quotes:

**Rotate every 6,250 miles (10,000 km), or whenever the tread depth difference between tires reaches 2/32 inch (1.5 mm) or more, whichever comes first.**

The Tire Industry Association recommends rotating tires at a regular interval, and for a Tesla the interval to follow is the one in Tesla's manual. Given that EVs tend to wear tires faster than comparable gas cars (see [Tesla tire basics](/learn/tesla/tesla-tire-basics)), the second half of the rule matters as much as the mileage. If you measure a 2/32 inch gap between tires before 6,250 miles, it's time.

Tesla's manual explains the reasoning: the car rides and handles best when all four tires are the same age, model and tread depth, and regular rotation keeps them wearing at the same rate.

## Staggered Teslas can't rotate front to back

Several Tesla wheel packages use wider tires on the rear than the front. Our [factory tire sizes](/learn/tesla/tesla-tire-sizes) guide marks which ones. On those cars:

- Front and rear tires are different sizes, so they **can't swap axles**.
- Whether side-to-side moves are possible depends on the tires. A tire with a directional tread can't simply move to the other side of the car.
- Without front-to-back rotation, the two axles can wear at different rates. Check tread depth on both at every service. You may end up replacing fronts and rears at different times.

Ask your installer what applies to your exact tires before the work starts.

## TPMS after a rotation or tire change

Most Teslas use sensors inside each wheel to report that tire's pressure. When tires move, the car needs to know.

**After a rotation, replacement or swap.** Tesla's manual says to update the car's tire configuration from the touchscreen: in the Service menu, under the wheel and tire settings. Menu names can change with software updates, so check the current manual for your car if the screen looks different.

**After a wheel change.** Tesla's manual says that after one or more wheels are replaced (not just the tires), the sensors relearn automatically within about 10 minutes of driving above 15 mph (25 km/h).

**If a warning stays on.** Tesla's manual says tires or wheels that don't match the original specification can stop the TPMS working properly, and that you should check for a TPMS malfunction warning after replacing tires or wheels. A warning that won't clear needs a technician with a TPMS tool. Our [TPMS light guide](/learn/pressure/tpms-light) explains the difference between a low-pressure warning and a system fault.

One thing TPMS doesn't do is replace a gauge. Tesla's manual says not to depend on the sensors to judge pressure, and NHTSA's guidance for every car is to check pressure monthly, with the tires cold, against the door placard.

## Signs your Tesla needs an alignment

Tesla's manual says to have the wheel alignment checked if you notice:

- **Uneven wear on one side of a tire**, the inner or outer edge wearing faster than the rest, or wear that's abnormally fast.
- **The car pulling** left or right while you hold the steering wheel straight.

Other moments worth an alignment check: after a hard pothole or curb hit, after suspension work, and when you fit a new set of tires so they start out even. An off-center steering wheel when driving straight is also a reason to have it looked at.

## Lifting a Tesla: leave it to a shop

A tire rotation means lifting the car, and a Tesla is not a car to put on a scissor jack in the driveway. Tesla's owner's manuals carry specific warnings:

- **Only Tesla's approved lift points.** The manual says never to put lift pads under the battery or the side rails. The battery pack sits under the floor, and lifting in the wrong place can damage it.
- **Jack Mode on air-suspension cars.** On cars with air suspension, the manual says Jack Mode has to be turned on before lifting. Otherwise the car may try to level itself, which Tesla warns can cause serious damage, injury or death.
- **Never lift while the charge cable is connected,** even if the car isn't charging.

We're not going to give step-by-step lifting instructions here. The right lift points and pad type vary by model, and the consequences of getting it wrong are serious. Have a tire shop or Tesla service lift the car, and if you're curious how it's done, read the lifting section of your own owner's manual.
