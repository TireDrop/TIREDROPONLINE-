## Yes, faster. Here's how much, and what to do about it

If your first set of EV tires didn't last as long as the tires on your old gas car, you're not imagining it. Michelin says EVs wear tires **about 20% faster on average** than comparable combustion vehicles.

That doesn't mean you're stuck with it. Wear is predictable once you measure it, and a few habits slow it down. This post is about planning for the next set and stretching the current one. For what makes an EV tire different in the first place, see our Learn guide on [EV tires](/learn/buying/ev-tires).

## Why EVs are harder on tires

Michelin points to two causes:

1. **Weight.** The battery pack makes an EV heavy for its size, and that weight presses on every tire on every mile.
2. **Instant torque.** Electric motors deliver full torque from a standstill. Hard launches put extra twisting stress on the driven tires and scrub rubber off the tread.

South Florida adds a third factor you can't change: hot pavement for much of the year. Our guide to [Florida heat and tires](/learn/florida/florida-heat-tires) explains what heat does.

## Budget with your own numbers, not an average

A 20% average says little about your car. Your own measurements say a lot. Here's a simple way to forecast your next set, with no guesswork about price:

1. **Measure tread depth** in three grooves on each tire, and note the lowest reading and your odometer.
2. **Measure again at the next rotation.** Note the miles driven between readings.
3. **Work out your wear rate:** 32nds lost divided by miles driven.
4. **Project forward** to 4/32, the point where AAA recommends replacing for wet roads.

**An example, with made-up numbers:** you measure 8/32 at 10,000 miles and 7/32 at 16,000 miles. That's 1/32 every 6,000 miles. From 7/32, you have about 3/32 to go before 4/32, or roughly 18,000 more miles. If you drive 1,000 miles a month, plan to replace in about a year and a half.

Redo the math at every rotation. If one axle is wearing faster, you'll see it early, while there's still time to fix the cause.

## The habits that stretch a set

### Accelerate smoothly

Michelin says smooth, progressive acceleration rather than hard launches reduces EV tire wear in measurable ways. It's the single habit that's most specific to EVs. If your car offers a gentler acceleration setting, it does the smoothing for you.

### Check pressure monthly, against the placard

Michelin recommends checking pressure manually at least once a month, using the number on your door jamb sticker, not the maximum printed on the sidewall. Low pressure increases rolling resistance, which costs range, and it changes how the tread meets the road. Check cold, before driving.

### Rotate on schedule

Rotation keeps wear even across all four tires, so they reach the end together. Follow your vehicle maker's interval. Tesla, for example, recommends rotating the Model Y every **6,250 miles (10,000 km)**, or whenever the tread depth difference reaches **2/32 in (1.5 mm)** or more, whichever comes first.

Some EVs use staggered sizes front and rear, which limits rotation options. Our [Tesla rotation, alignment and TPMS guide](/learn/tesla/tesla-rotation-alignment-tpms) covers that case.

### Keep the alignment in check

Hitting a curb or a deep pothole can knock alignment out, and misalignment wears the inside or outside edge of a tire fast. If you see uneven wear across a tread, have the alignment checked.

## What wears an EV set early

Most early wear on an EV comes from a short list of causes:

- **Launches.** Instant torque is fun, and it costs tread. The driven axle shows it first.
- **Skipped rotations.** On a heavy car, an axle that wears faster pulls ahead quickly.
- **Low pressure.** A slow leak that goes unnoticed for weeks wears the shoulders and costs range.
- **Alignment knocked out by a curb or pothole.** Watch the inner edges, which are hard to see without turning the wheel.
- **Heat.** Florida pavement is hot for much of the year, and heat speeds up wear and aging. Our guide to [Florida heat and tires](/learn/florida/florida-heat-tires) covers what you can control.

None of these are unusual. Catching them at each rotation, with the log below, is what keeps a 20% average from becoming something worse.

Rotate even when the tires look even. On a heavy car, a difference of a 32nd or two between axles is hard to see by eye and easy to measure, and catching it early is what lets all four tires reach the replacement point together.

## Where not to economize

When the time comes, the replacement has to match the original specification:

- **Size, load index and speed rating** from your door placard.
- **XL or HL markings.** Michelin says that if your EV came on HL (high load) tires, every replacement must also be HL. An XL tire of the same size isn't a substitute.

Beyond that spec, EV-focused tires add features like low rolling resistance for range and sound-absorbing foam for a quieter cabin. Continental and Michelin both describe tires engineered for EV weight, torque and noise. Whether those are worth it depends on how much you value range and quiet.

## A tire log for EV owners

| At each rotation, record | Why |
|---|---|
| Date and odometer | Gives you miles per 32nd |
| Lowest tread reading per tire | Shows which axle wears faster |
| Pressure before adjusting | Catches slow leaks |
| Any uneven wear | Flags alignment early |
