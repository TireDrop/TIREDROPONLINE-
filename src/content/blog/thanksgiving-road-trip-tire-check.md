## The busiest drive of the year

Thanksgiving is when South Florida loads up the car and points it north: up I-95 to family in Jacksonville or Georgia, up the Turnpike to Orlando, or across to the Gulf coast. You're not alone. For Thanksgiving 2025, AAA projected **81.8 million** people would travel at least 50 miles from home, about **73 million of them by car**, nearly 90% of all travelers. AAA publishes a new forecast each November.

A fully loaded car, hours at highway speed and a chance of rain on the way is exactly when tires matter most. The check below takes about 15 minutes. Do it a week or two before you leave, so there's time to fix anything you find.

## Step 1: Pressure, cold

Check pressure when the tires are cold. USTMA defines cold as parked for **at least three hours**. Use the number on the **placard on the driver's door pillar** or in your owner's manual, not the maximum printed on the sidewall. Our [sidewall max PSI post](/blog/sidewall-max-psi-myth) explains why.

NHTSA recommends checking pressure before long trips and before carrying heavy loads. A holiday drive is both. Two extra points:

- **Check after you load the car,** if your owner's manual lists a different pressure for a full load.
- **Expect the gauge to move.** Pressure rises as tires warm up on the highway and drops on a cool morning up north. Only cold readings count.

## Step 2: Tread, in three grooves

Measure tread depth in an outer, a center and an inner groove on every tire. USTMA's penny test is the quick version: put a penny upside down in a groove, and if you can see all of Lincoln's head, it's time for new tires. A tread gauge gives you an actual number.

For a long drive, use **4/32** as your line, not the 2/32 wear bars. AAA's testing found tires worn to 4/32 of an inch took an average of **87 feet longer** to stop on wet pavement than new tires, and AAA recommends replacing at 4/32. If rain is forecast anywhere along the route, that difference matters. Our guide to [checking tread depth](/learn/tread/how-to-check-tread-depth) shows each method.

Also look at how the tread is wearing. Wear on one edge or in the center usually points to alignment or pressure; get it checked before a long drive, not after.

## Step 3: Sidewalls and tread surface

Walk around the car and look at both sides of each tire if you can:

- **Bulges or bubbles** in the sidewall. That tire needs replacing, not a road trip. Our guide to [sidewall bubbles](/learn/damage/tire-bubble-sidewall) explains why.
- **Cuts, cracks or scrapes** from curbs.
- **Nails or screws** in the tread. A small tread puncture can often be repaired properly before you go; see [Can My Tire Be Repaired?](/can-my-tire-be-repaired)

## Step 4: The spare, or what you have instead

Many drivers find out what's in the trunk on the shoulder of I-95. Find out in your driveway instead.

- **A compact spare ("donut").** Check its pressure; it usually needs more than your regular tires, and the number is on its label. Michelin says not to exceed **50 mph** on one. It's for reaching a shop, not finishing the trip.
- **A full-size spare.** Check its pressure and its age. A spare can be as old as the car.
- **No spare at all.** AAA found **nearly one-third of new vehicles** were sold without one, with a sealant and inflator kit or run-flat tires instead. Read the kit's instructions now.

Our [spare tire types guide](/learn/damage/spare-tire-types) explains each option and its limits.

## Step 5: The load

A holiday car is a heavy car: passengers, luggage, gifts, maybe a cooler or a roof box. The **door placard lists the maximum combined weight** of people and cargo your vehicle should carry. Stay within it, and if your manual lists a loaded pressure, use it.

## The pre-trip checklist

| Check | When | What you're looking for |
|---|---|---|
| Pressure, all four plus spare | Cold, morning of departure | Door placard number |
| Tread depth | A week or two before | 4/32 or more in every groove |
| Sidewalls | A week or two before | No bulges, cuts or cracks |
| Spare or kit | A week or two before | Inflated, in date, and you know how to use it |
| Load | While packing | Within the placard's maximum |

## On the road

- **Recheck pressure cold** at your destination before the drive home.
- **If the car pulls, vibrates or the TPMS light comes on,** slow down and stop somewhere sensible to look. Our guide to the [TPMS light](/learn/pressure/tpms-light) explains solid vs flashing.
- **Rain on the way?** Slow down and leave more room. Our post on [driving through heavy downpours](/blog/hydroplaning-broward-downpours) covers hydroplaning.

## If a tire goes on the highway

Most trips end without trouble, but it's worth having the plan in your head before you need it:

1. **Hold the wheel firmly** and keep the car straight.
2. **Ease off the gas** and let the car slow down gradually.
3. **Signal and move over** when it's clear, to a spot well away from traffic: an exit, a service plaza or a wide shoulder.
4. **Turn on your hazard lights,** and keep everyone out of the travel lanes.
5. **Call roadside assistance** if changing the tire there isn't practical.

If you used a compact spare or a repair kit, treat it as a way to reach a tire shop, not to finish the trip.
