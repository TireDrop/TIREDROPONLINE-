## The family crossover question

The CR-V spends a lot of its life full: kids, car seats, beach chairs, a week of groceries, a suitcase for every family member on the drive to Orlando. That's the angle most "best tires for CR-V" lists skip, and it's where this guide starts.

We don't rank brands. Instead, here's what Honda's own manual says, what a full load means for tire choice, and how to judge wet grip for South Florida.

## Step 1: Honda's replacement rules

Honda's CR-V owner's manual is specific. Replacement tires should be **radials of the same size, load range, speed rating and maximum cold tire pressure rating** as the originals, as shown on the sidewall. It also says to use the size and type of tires the manual recommends.

The reason is spelled out too. Honda warns that tires of a different size or construction can cause the **ABS, vehicle stability assist (VSA), hill start assist and the AWD system to malfunction**. Modern crossovers read wheel speeds constantly, and mismatched tires confuse them.

If you're replacing wheels as well, Honda says the wheel specifications should match the originals, and to use only TPMS-specified wheels approved for the vehicle.

## Step 2: All four, or matched pairs

Honda's manual says it's **best to replace all four tires at the same time**. If that isn't possible, replace the **front or rear tires in pairs**.

On an AWD CR-V, take the "all four" advice seriously. A new tire is taller than a worn one, and an AWD system notices differences between axles. Before you replace just two:

1. Measure the remaining tires in three grooves each.
2. Compare them with the new tire's tread depth.
3. Check your manual for any limits.

## Step 3: Loaded for a road trip

Every vehicle's door placard states the most it should carry. Under federal standard **FMVSS No. 110**, the Tire and Loading Information placard lists the vehicle's seating capacity and the maximum combined weight of occupants and cargo, along with the tire size and cold pressure.

What that means when you shop:

- **Never go below the original load rating.** The load index on the new tire must be at or above what Honda specifies.
- **XL is about load, not quality.** Michelin explains that XL (extra load) tires are built to carry more weight at higher pressure than standard load tires of the same size. If your CR-V came on XL tires, replace them with XL. Our [XL vs SL guide](/learn/buying/xl-vs-sl-tires) explains the difference.
- **Pressure is part of capacity.** A tire's load rating assumes correct inflation. Check pressure cold before a loaded trip, using the placard.

Our [load index guide](/learn/sidewall/load-index) shows how to read the number on any tire.

## Step 4: Rain grip for Broward roads

A family car spends a lot of time on wet school runs from May to October. Grip in the rain depends on two things you can check:

- **The tire's wet-braking credentials.** The UTQG traction grade (AA, A, B or C) is a straight-line wet braking signal; independent test results add hydroplaning and handling. See our [UTQG guide](/learn/sidewall/utqg-ratings).
- **How much tread is left.** AAA's tests found tires worn to 4/32 of an inch took an average of 87 feet longer to stop on wet pavement than new ones. AAA recommends replacing at 4/32.

## Step 5: Hybrid or gas, same rules

The CR-V Hybrid follows the same rules: match the size, load range, speed rating and pressure rating Honda specifies. Hybrid owners often care about rolling resistance because it affects fuel economy, so it's worth asking about once the spec is met. Correct pressure matters too; under-inflation costs mileage on any car.

## Mistakes we'd steer you away from

- **Picking a tire by size alone.** Honda's manual asks for the same load range, speed rating and pressure rating too.
- **Mixing tire constructions or sizes.** Honda warns it can make the ABS, VSA and AWD systems malfunction.
- **Packing for a road trip without checking pressure.** A full car on under-inflated tires builds heat. Check cold, before you load up.
- **Waiting for the wear bars before rainy season.** Plan around 4/32, not 2/32. Our post on [why 4/32 matters in Florida rain](/blog/worn-tires-florida-rain-4-32) explains why.
- **Swapping wheels without checking TPMS.** Honda says to use only TPMS-specified wheels approved for the vehicle.

## Before a loaded road trip

A few minutes in the driveway before the drive to Orlando or the Keys:

1. **Check pressure cold** on all four tires, plus the spare if you have one, against the door placard.
2. **Look at the load.** People plus luggage plus anything on a roof rack or hitch counts toward the placard's maximum.
3. **Measure the tread** if it's been a while; long highway runs in summer rain are exactly where 4/32 matters.
4. **Scan the sidewalls** for cuts or bulges from curbs and parking garages.

## Keep it even

- **Rotate** on the schedule in your owner's manual or maintenance reminders. If none is given, USTMA's fallback is every 5,000 to 8,000 miles. Our [rotation tool](/tire-rotation-pattern) shows the pattern.
- **Check pressure monthly,** cold.
- **Register your tires** and check recalls on NHTSA's site.
