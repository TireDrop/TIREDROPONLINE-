## Two good options, different strengths

We sell tires online and we fit them in a shop, so we see this question from both sides. The honest answer is that neither is always better. Each one is strong at different things, and the right choice depends on what you care about and where you live.

Here are the trade-offs, how to compare cost without being fooled by a tire-only price, and the steps that matter no matter where you buy.

## Where buying online is strong

**Choice.** An online store isn't limited to what fits on one shop's racks. TireDrop, for example, ships from distributor warehouses, so the catalog isn't capped by what's in one building.

**Comparing at your own pace.** You can line up two or three tires and read the specs side by side: size, load index, speed rating and the UTQG grades for treadwear, traction and temperature. NHTSA's consumer guide explains that the UTQG grades are assigned by the tire makers themselves, so treat them as one input, not the verdict. TireDrop's [compare tool](/compare) shows each tire's full spec sheet.

**Shipping to where it suits you.** Home, work or an installer. TireDrop ships free to addresses in the 48 contiguous states and DC.

**No pressure at the counter.** You can research on a Sunday night and order when you're ready.

## Where a local shop is strong

**It sees your car.** A technician can look at the old tires, spot the wear pattern, and tell you if it points to alignment or suspension. That's hard to do from a product page.

**One visit.** If the shop has your size on hand, buying and fitting can happen in the same stop. If not, it orders them and you come back.

**One place to call.** If something's wrong after the install, the shop that sold and fitted the tires is the single point of contact.

**Local knowledge.** A shop in your area knows the roads, the rain and the potholes on your commute.

## The hybrid most people end up using

A lot of drivers now split the job: buy online, install locally. It works well if you plan the installation before you order.

In South Florida, that's how TireDrop is built. It's the online store of Extreme Tires in Sunrise, so you can:

- choose **free ship-to-store** at checkout and have the tires fitted at the Sunrise shop, or
- have a **mobile van** fit them at your home or office inside the local install area.

Outside South Florida, the order ships to you and you take the tires to an installer you trust. Call that installer first and ask two things: whether they fit tires the customer brings in, and what they charge to do it. Our post on [getting tires installed after buying online](/blog/bought-tires-online-install-broward) walks through each route.

## Get the size right before anything else

Online or local, the tires have to fit your car. USTMA's guidance is that replacement tires match the **size, load index and speed rating** on your vehicle placard, with a load index **equal to or above** what the placard requires. The placard is on the driver's door edge or door post.

## Compare the installed total, not the tire price

A tire-only price can make two options look further apart, or closer, than they are. Put both on the same footing by listing everything it takes to get the tires on the car:

| Line item | Buying online | Buying at a local shop |
|---|---|---|
| Tires | Listed price | Quoted price |
| Shipping | Check the store's terms (TireDrop: free to the 48 contiguous states and DC) | Not applicable |
| Mounting and balancing | Charged by your installer | Ask the shop |
| Valve stems and TPMS reset or relearn | Ask your installer | Ask the shop |
| Disposal of old tires | Ask your installer | Ask the shop |
| Florida new tire fee | $1 per new tire, listed separately | $1 per new tire, listed separately |

About that last line: Florida law puts a **$1 fee on each new tire sold at retail** in the state, and it must be **stated separately on the invoice** (Fla. Stat. § 403.718). Seeing it as its own line is normal.

Ask each side for the full, installed number before you choose.

## Timing

A shop that has your size in stock can often fit it on the spot. An online order needs shipping time, and the installation has to be arranged around delivery.

We don't print delivery promises. TireDrop's checkout shows a delivery estimate for your specific order once your address is in, because transit depends on which warehouse has your size. For ship-to-store orders, the Sunrise shop calls you when your tires are checked in, and you can book the fitting ahead of time.

If you need tires today because one just failed, that points to a local shop or a mobile service with your size on hand. If you're planning ahead, either route works.

## After you buy, wherever you bought

Two small jobs protect you later, and they apply to both routes.

**Keep the DOT numbers.** Photograph the full DOT number on each tire, or ask for them on your invoice.

**Make sure the tires are registered.** Federal rules under 49 CFR 574.8 require independent tire sellers to give each buyer a registration form with the tire identification numbers already recorded, and tire makers that offer it also accept registration online. That's how a recall notice finds you. You can also search tire recalls on NHTSA's recall site at any time.
