## No top five here

The RAV4 is one of the most common SUVs on South Florida roads, and "best tires for RAV4" pulls up plenty of ranked lists. We don't rank brands. What we can do is give you the checklist Toyota and the tire makers publish, so any tire you look at can be judged against it.

The RAV4-specific part comes down to two things: **getting the exact size for your trim, and handling all-wheel drive correctly.**

## Step 1: Find your exact size

There isn't one RAV4 tire size. Toyota has fitted different wheel sizes across model years and trims, so a neighbor's RAV4 may not take the same tire as yours.

Start at the car:

- **The door placard.** The Tire and Loading Information label on the driver's door pillar shows the original size, load and cold pressure.
- **The owner's manual.** Toyota's manuals are on its [owners site](https://www.toyota.com/owners/warranty-owners-manuals/).
- **The current tires.** Useful to confirm, but a previous owner may have changed wheels.

Toyota's manuals ask for a replacement tire of the **same size and construction, with the same or greater maximum load** as the originals. USTMA gives the same rule for every vehicle: match the placard's size, load-carrying capacity and speed rating.

## Step 2: If your RAV4 is AWD, match all four

Many RAV4s are all-wheel drive. That changes the tire rules.

An AWD system drives all four wheels and expects them to turn at nearly the same rate. A new tire is taller than a worn one of the same size, so mismatched tires turn at different rates. Bridgestone warns that on some AWD vehicles, big diameter differences, such as two heavily worn tires against two new ones, can lead to excessive wear on drivetrain components.

Toyota's guidance for AWD models, from its owner's manuals:

- **All four tires should be the same brand and tread pattern.**
- **Don't mix** brands, sizes, constructions or tread patterns.

That's why the brand you pick for one tire is the brand you're picking for all four.

## Step 3: Replace in a set, or at least in pairs

Toyota's tire warranty guide recommends replacing **all four tires, or at least both front or both rear tires, as a set**. It also notes that a tire worn to the built-in indicators at 2/32 of an inch must be replaced.

If one tire is damaged early on an AWD RAV4:

1. **Ask whether it can be repaired.** Our [repair checker](/can-my-tire-be-repaired) shows where a puncture can and can't be fixed.
2. **Measure the other three** in three grooves each.
3. **Check your manual** for any tread-depth guidance.
4. **If the others are well worn,** replacing a pair or all four keeps the set matched.

Our myth check on [replacing all four tires](/blog/replace-all-four-tires-myth) compares what different tire and vehicle makers say.

## Step 4: Choose for Florida rain, not snow

RAV4 owners up north debate winter grip. Here, the job is wet braking in summer storms, heat, and long highway miles.

- **Tire type.** Check what type your RAV4 is on now; the sidewall and the original-tire listing in your manual will tell you. Our guide to [all-season vs all-weather tires](/learn/buying/all-season-vs-all-weather-tires) explains when the other type makes sense, which in South Florida is rarely about snow.
- **UTQG traction grade.** It reflects straight-line wet braking, graded AA, A, B or C. Our [UTQG guide](/learn/sidewall/utqg-ratings) explains its limits.
- **Tread depth over time.** Wet grip fades as tread wears. AAA recommends replacing at 4/32, before the 2/32 wear bars.
- **Hybrid owners:** match the placard first. Low rolling resistance can help fuel economy, so ask about it when comparing tires.

Highway miles add one more factor. Long runs on I-95 and the Turnpike in summer heat are hard on tires that are low on air, because an under-inflated tire flexes more and builds more heat. Check pressure cold, before the drive, against the door placard, and again before any trip with a full load of people and luggage.

## Step 5: Keep the set even

On AWD, even wear protects both the tires and the drivetrain.

- **Rotate on your manual's schedule.** If it doesn't give one, USTMA recommends every 5,000 to 8,000 miles.
- **Check pressure monthly,** cold, against the door placard, not the sidewall number.
- **Watch for uneven wear** across a tread. It points to alignment or pressure.
- **Register your new tires** and check recalls by tire on NHTSA's site.

## Mistakes we'd steer you away from

- **Buying by "RAV4 size" from a forum.** Sizes change across years and trims. Use your placard.
- **Replacing one tire on an AWD model without measuring the others.** Toyota's guidance is a set, or at least a matched pair, and the same brand and tread pattern on AWD.
- **Mixing a new pair with two different tires on AWD.** Even if the size matches, a different brand or tread pattern goes against Toyota's AWD guidance.
- **Filling to the sidewall number.** The placard pressure is the target. See our [sidewall max psi myth](/blog/sidewall-max-psi-myth) post.
- **Skipping rotation.** Uneven wear is what turns a single flat into a four-tire bill on an AWD vehicle.
