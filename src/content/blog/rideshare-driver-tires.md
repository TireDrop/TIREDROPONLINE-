## Your car is your workplace. Your tires are a running cost.

For most drivers, tires are something you think about every few years. For a full-time Uber or Lyft driver running airport trips to FLL and MIA and cruise passengers to Port Everglades, tires are a line item, like fuel.

That changes the question. It's not "which tire is best?" It's **"which tire, at what pressure, replaced when, keeps the cost per mile down and the car on the road?"** Here's the math.

## How fast rideshare miles use up tread

Bridgestone puts a good-quality all-season tire at roughly **three to five years** for a driver covering **12,000 to 15,000 miles a year**.

A full-time rideshare driver can cover that in a few months. So a few things follow:

- **Tread, not age, will almost always end your tires' life.** The age guidance in our [how long tires last](/learn/tread/how-long-do-tires-last) guide matters less to you than to a weekend driver.
- **Small differences in wear rate add up fast.** A tire that wears 10% slower saves you more sets per year than it would save a commuter.
- **Maintenance intervals come around quickly.** Rotation every 5,000 to 8,000 miles could mean every month or two.

## The pressure math

This is the cheapest gain available, and it's measured.

Fueleconomy.gov, run by the U.S. Department of Energy and EPA, says:

- Keeping tires at the correct pressure improves gas mileage by **0.6% on average, up to 3% in some cases**.
- Under-inflated tires lower gas mileage by about **0.2% for every 1 psi drop** in the average pressure of all four tires.

Low pressure also wears the tread unevenly and builds heat. Rideshare cars do a lot of stop-start city driving and long highway runs, often in the heat of the day, so check pressure at least monthly, cold, against the door placard.

## The rotation math

Rotation keeps all four tires wearing at the same rate, so they reach the end together instead of one axle forcing an early replacement.

USTMA recommends following your owner's manual, and if no interval is given, rotating every **5,000 to 8,000 miles**.

| Miles per week | 5,000-mile interval | 8,000-mile interval |
|---|---|---|
| 500 | About every 10 weeks | About every 16 weeks |
| 1,000 | About every 5 weeks | About every 8 weeks |
| 1,500 | About every 3 weeks | About every 5 weeks |

Tie it to something you already do, like an oil change, so it doesn't slip. Our [rotation pattern tool](/tire-rotation-pattern) shows the right pattern for your drivetrain.

## Choosing tires for high mileage

Start with the door placard: size, load index and speed rating. Then compare tires that fit on what matters for your work.

- **Treadwear warranty.** The maker's mileage promise is the easiest tread-life number to compare across brands. Read the conditions; most require regular rotation and records. Goodyear's tread life warranty page shows how these terms are typically set out.
- **UTQG treadwear grade.** NHTSA's grade is relative and assigned by the manufacturer, so compare it within one brand.
- **UTQG traction grade.** A wet-braking signal (AA, A, B or C). Rain is part of the job here from May to October.
- **Comfort and noise.** Your passengers notice. Touring all-season tires are generally designed with comfort in mind.

Our guide to [choosing between tire brands](/blog/choosing-between-tire-brands) walks through comparing tires without the hype.

## When to replace: plan around 4/32

In AAA's wet-braking tests, tires worn to 4/32 of an inch took an average of 87 feet longer to stop than new tires. AAA recommends replacing at 4/32. NHTSA's end-of-life line is 2/32.

For a driver, 4/32 is also the right planning line for a business reason: it gives you time to order and book an install during a quiet stretch, instead of losing a busy Friday to a worn or damaged tire. Our post on [why 4/32 matters in Florida rain](/blog/worn-tires-florida-rain-4-32) explains the numbers.

## What airport and city driving does to tires

Rideshare miles aren't only highway miles. Pickup lanes at FLL and MIA, cruise terminal curbs at Port Everglades and tight hotel driveways all mean low-speed turns close to curbs. A scuffed sidewall is easy to miss and worth a look: a cut or bulge in the sidewall usually can't be repaired, and our [repair checker](/can-my-tire-be-repaired) shows why.

City potholes and curb strikes can also knock the alignment out, which shows up as wear on one edge of the tread. Catching it after a few thousand miles is a lot cheaper than finding it when a tire is worn through on the inside.

## A 10-minute monthly routine

Put this on the calendar for the first day of each month, before your first ride:

1. **Pressure, cold,** all four tires, against the door placard. Adjust before driving.
2. **Tread depth,** lowest groove on each tire. Write it down with the odometer reading.
3. **Sidewalls,** for cuts, bulges or cracks from curbs and airport pickup lanes.
4. **Even wear,** across each tread. One worn edge points to alignment.
5. **Spare or repair kit,** present and ready. Many newer cars don't carry a full spare.

Your notes double as the records most treadwear warranties ask for. Our [Do I Need Tires Yet?](/tire-check) tool turns the tread readings into a plain answer.
