## The drive half of South Florida knows

Theme parks, the airport, a college visit, family for the holidays: for a lot of Broward and Palm Beach drivers, the Turnpike north to Orlando is a regular trip. It's a long stretch of highway at speed, often with a full car, often in the heat, and sometimes through an afternoon storm.

None of that is a problem for tires in good shape. This post covers the check to do before you go, the stops along the way, and what to do if a tire goes on the Turnpike.

## Before you leave: the 15-minute tire check

Do this in the driveway a few days ahead, so there's time to fix anything you find.

1. **Pressure, cold.** Check all four tires and the spare against the **driver's door placard**, not the sidewall maximum. USTMA defines cold as parked for at least three hours. If your owner's manual lists a pressure for a fully loaded car, use it.
2. **Tread depth.** Measure in three grooves on every tire. AAA recommends replacing at **4/32 inch**; its testing found tires at that depth took an average of 87 feet longer to stop on wet pavement than new ones.
3. **Sidewalls.** Look for bulges, cuts and cracks.
4. **The spare, or what replaced it.** AAA says about 30 percent of new cars come without a spare, carrying a sealant kit or run-flat tires instead. Know which you have.
5. **The load.** The door placard also lists the most weight your car should carry in people and cargo. A family plus luggage adds up quickly.

Our [road trip tire check](/blog/thanksgiving-road-trip-tire-check) goes through each step in more detail.

## The stops, by milepost

Florida's Turnpike counts mileposts from the south. Heading north from Broward, its service plaza page lists:

| Plaza | Milepost |
|---|---|
| Pompano Beach | 65 |
| West Palm Beach | 94 |
| Port St. Lucie / Fort Pierce | 144 |
| Fort Drum | 184 |
| Canoe Creek | 229 |
| Turkey Lake (Orlando) | 263 |

Starting from Miami-Dade, there's also **Snapper Creek at milepost 19**, a smaller convenience store with fuel.

What the Turnpike says the plazas offer:

- **Fuel around the clock.** All plaza stores with fuel are open 24 hours a day, with regular gasoline grades, diesel and E85.
- **EV charging,** with fees that vary. Tesla owners use Tesla Superchargers at the plazas.
- **Restaurants, restrooms, ATMs, WiFi** and enclosed dog parks.

The Turnpike also describes **Turkey Lake** as only the second location in the US offering passenger-car drivers its WheelRight tire-check technology. If you pass through, it's an easy second look at your tires before the drive home.

Amenities change, so check the Turnpike's plaza page before you leave. This list reflects it as of October 2026.

## Using the stops for your tires

A stop is a good moment for a 30-second walk-around:

- **Look at each tire.** Anything that looks lower than the others, or anything stuck in the tread?
- **Remember the heat.** Tires get warm on the highway, and warm tires read higher. Don't let air out of a hot tire to match the placard; that leaves it low once it cools.
- **Check the dash.** If the TPMS light came on during the drive, check that tire with a gauge at the plaza and add air if it's low. Our [TPMS light guide](/learn/pressure/tpms-light) explains solid vs flashing.

## Rain on the way

Between May 15 and October 15, the National Weather Service in Miami's wet season, an afternoon drive north has a good chance of running into a storm somewhere along the route. Heavy rain on a fast highway is where tread depth shows up: a worn tire can't clear water as well, and the car can start to float on standing water.

If it pours, slow down, leave more room, keep your headlights on and avoid the outer edges of the lane where water collects. If the car starts to feel light in the steering, ease off the gas and keep the wheel straight. Our post on [hydroplaning in Broward downpours](/blog/hydroplaning-broward-downpours) covers it in more detail.

## If a tire goes on the Turnpike

1. **Hold the wheel firmly and ease off the gas.** Don't brake hard.
2. **Signal and get to the right shoulder** or the next plaza or exit if the car can roll there at low speed.
3. **Hazard lights on.** Under Florida's Move Over law, as FLHSMV describes it, drivers must move over or slow down for a vehicle stopped with its hazards on, flat tire included.
4. **Dial \*347.**

Florida's Turnpike runs its own **Road Rangers** patrol on the mainline, **mileposts 0 to 308, 24 hours a day, seven days a week**, and calls the service free. The services it lists:

- Tire changes **with your vehicle's spare**
- Emergency gas, up to 2 gallons
- Jump starts
- Water refills
- Minor emergency repairs

That first item is the catch: no spare means no tire change. If your car has a sealant kit or run-flats, know their limits before the trip. Our [spare tire types guide](/learn/damage/spare-tire-types) explains them, and our post on [a flat tire on I-95](/blog/flat-tire-on-i-95) covers the roadside steps in more detail.

## The drive home

Before you head back south, repeat the cold pressure check in the hotel parking lot in the morning. If you used a spare or a sealant kit on the way up, get to a tire shop before the long drive home; a donut is for reaching a shop, not for the whole drive home.
